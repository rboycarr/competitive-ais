## Deliberately out of scope (P1-50)
Ed25519-signed manifests exported to the audit-anchor bucket, verification against them, supplementary checkpoints for `late_commit`, key rotation. Until then an attacker who can rewrite both rows and checkpoint rows is not detected (documented in `docs/02` §3.1).

## Review focus
1. Can an audit row be changed, deleted or inserted by the app or retention account, or can verification miss a change made without rewriting the checkpoints?
2. Canonical hashing: is the serialization stable across the MySQL JSON round trip, character sets and integer types? Any ambiguity or collision between fields?
3. Checkpoint ranges under concurrency: open transactions, rolled-back IDs, two runs at once, clock and lag-window assumptions.
4. Context allowlist: can secrets, tokens or prompts reach the table?
5. Transaction rule: can an audit row commit without its business change, or the reverse?
