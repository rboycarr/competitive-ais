## Objective
P1-31a: a director reviews and decides a source's policy. PO decisions 2026-11-03:
- P1-31 is delivered in two slices. This one covers submitting, deciding and the policy history; P1-31b (suspend, reinstate, retire) comes next.
- O9: the CI Director decides (`source.approve`, which counts only with two-factor authentication). There is no counsel sign-off in the app.
- O30: a rejection returns the source to its proposer, as "needs info" does, and the source can be submitted again.

What changed:
- **Schema** (`modules/Sources/database/migrations/2026_11_16_000100_create_source_policies_table.php`):
  - **`source_policies` table:** one row per decision, grant class `append_only` (already listed in `config/database.php`).
  - **`proposed_by` copy:** each row copies the source's proposer through the composite foreign key (source_id, proposed_by) → sources (id, proposed_by), with ON UPDATE RESTRICT. A CHECK `decided_by <> proposed_by` then stops anyone deciding their own proposal.
  - **Other CHECKs:**
    - decision and robots values; version ≥ 1;
    - the terms URL shape;
    - positive limits;
    - an approval needs a terms summary and a review date;
    - a rejection or needs-info needs notes;
    - personal data needs a lawful basis.
  - **On `sources`:**
    - `current_policy_id`;
    - a stored generated `current_policy_decision` (`'approved'` whenever a policy is set);
    - the composite foreign key (current_policy_id, id, current_policy_decision) → source_policies (id, source_id, decision), so the policy in force is an approval of the same source;
    - `sources_current_policy_check`: an approved or suspended source has a policy.
  - **Rollback:** refused while decisions exist.
- **`SourcePolicyService`:**
  - **`submit`:** needs `source.propose` and must be the owner or proposer. Moves `proposed` → `policy_review`.
  - **`decide`:** needs `source.approve` and must not be the proposer. It appends version n + 1. The source becomes `approved` with `current_policy_id`, or returns to `proposed`.
  - **Every step:**
    - locks the source row and checks visibility again (404 if hidden) and the status;
    - a decision names the version it was based on (`based_on_version`), so it is refused if the history changed.
  - **Robots rule:** HTTP fetch and headless browser sources need robots `allowed` to be approved.
  - **Audit:** `source.submitted` and `source.policy_decided` (codes only).
- **HTTP:**
  - `POST /sources/{source}/submit` (`can:source.propose`);
  - `POST /sources/{source}/policies` (`can:source.approve`) with `DecideSourcePolicyRequest`;
  - the detail page shows the policy history, the submit button and the decision form.
- **`SourceService::host`:** now takes the field and label, so the terms URL follows the base-URL rules.

## Review focus
1. **Can a source be approved, or its policy changed, without an independent director's decision?** Look at:
   - the proposer rule (application and database), the owner versus the proposer, and a director proposing then deciding;
   - users without two-factor;
   - hidden sources;
   - any path that changes `current_policy_id` or `status`.
2. **Schema and grants:**
   - the composite foreign keys, including the stored generated column in a foreign key on MySQL 8.4;
   - the CHECKs and the append-only grant;
   - the circular references between `sources` and `source_policies`;
   - the rollback, and whether `down()` leaves anything behind.
3. **Concurrency and stale decisions:** the lock order, `based_on_version`, and parallel decisions.
4. **Validation and output:**
   - the terms URL, dates and text limits;
   - how the history is rendered (the links, text shown as text).
5. **Tests:** gaps in `SourcePolicyTest`, `SourcePolicyConcurrencyTest` and `Sources.test.ts`. Please mark anything that is not a merge blocker as such.
